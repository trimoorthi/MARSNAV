Traverse Slope Hazard Index

SAFETY THRESHOLDS

Loose basaltic regolith drastically reduces tire traction and boot stability on inclined gradients. Exceeding slope limits risks catastrophic rover rollover or suit puncture.

0° – 12° : Safe Traverse

Unrestricted astronaut EVA and pressurized rover transit. Standard power consumption.

12° – 20° : Cautionary Zone

Reduced speed (0.5 km/h). 220% increase in mechanical slip. Requires traction telemetry monitoring.

> 20° : Critical Incline / No-Go

Exceeds regolith angle of repose (30–35°). High risk of unrecoverable scree avalanche.

Source: HiRISE Digital Terrain Models (DTM) / NASA JPL Rover Operations

Cosmic & Solar Radiation

CRITICAL HAZARD

Mars lacks a global intrinsic dipole magnetic field and has a thin atmosphere, exposing surface astronauts to Galactic Cosmic Rays (GCR) and Solar Particle Events (SPE).

SURVIVAL PROTOCOLS:
Seek regolith-shielded habitats (≥3 meters of packed soil) or natural basalt lava tubes during solar proton storms.
Wear active dosimeters; limit lifetime EVA radiation budget to ≤600 mSv.
Source: Mars Odyssey MARIE / MSL RAD Telemetry

Global Dust Storms & Electrostatics

HIGH RISK

Dust storms can envelop the entire planet for months, cutting solar irradiance by up to 99% and creating electrostatic charges capable of shorting rover electronics.

SURVIVAL PROTOCOLS:
Monitor optical depth τ; abort non-essential EVAs when τ exceeds 1.5.
Engage automated electrostatic dust-repulsion coatings on solar collectors and habitat airlocks.
Source: NASA Mars Climate Database (LMD) / MRO MCS

Toxic Perchlorates in Regolith

CHEMICAL HAZARD

Martian soil contains 0.5% to 1.0% toxic perchlorate salts ($ClO_4^-$), which disrupt human thyroid function and cause lung tissue damage if tracked into habitat living areas.

SURVIVAL PROTOCOLS:
Employ external Suitport airlocks so EVA suits never enter the pressurized living quarters.
Deploy catalytic biochemical wash systems to reduce perchlorates to chlorine and breathable oxygen.
Source: Phoenix Mars Lander WCL / MSL SAM Instrument

Transparent Route Risk Scoring Architecture

ALGORITHM EXPLANATION & WEIGHTINGS

MARSNAV calculates a composite risk index from $0$ (optimal) to $100$ (no-go) based on four verifiable physical factors. If authentic data is unavailable for a given sector, the factor is designated as Data Unavailable (Unpenalized) instead of assigning invented metrics.

1. Incline Gradient (0 – 30 pts): Derived from MOLA elevation delta. Gradients >12° incur steepness penalties; >20° exceeds the angle of repose.
2. Known Hazard Intersections (0 – 40 pts): Calculated via great-circle polygon containment. Crossing scarps, chasms, or active dust alleys triggers immediate risk escalation.
3. Distance & Life Support Margin (0 – 20 pts): Based on EVA suit oxygen consumption (0.035 kg/hr/person) and thermal battery power reserves.
4. Data Coverage & Uncertainty Buffer (0 – 10 pts): Regions lacking sub-meter HiRISE or radar sounding carry an uncertainty penalty reflecting blind-spot risks.

First successful Mars surface operation. Global photographic mosaic establishing baseline geodesy.

2001
MGS MOLA & Mars Odyssey
High-precision laser altimetry and epithermal neutron detection of widespread polar ice.
2006
MRO HiRISE & CRISM
Sub-meter surface imagery and mineral spectroscopy discovering ancient aqueous clay deposits.
2012
MSL Curiosity Rover
Gale Crater ancient habitable lakebed confirmation. Continuous REMS environmental monitoring.
2021+
Perseverance & MOXIE
Jezero Crater sample caching, in-situ $O_2$ generation, and preparation for human Marswalk EVAs.
